Privacy Policy

Simple Track — Calorie Log

Effective 2 September 2026

HomeSupport

The short version. Simple Track has no account system and no server. Your food diary, weights, recipes, and settings are stored on your iPhone. Nothing is collected about you, nothing is sold, and nothing is used for advertising. The only information the app ever sends anywhere is a barcode you choose to scan.

What is stored, and where

Everything below is stored on your iPhone, in the app's own storage area. It is not uploaded to Arosemena Apps, and there is no service to upload it to.

InformationWhy the app has it
Food you log, with amounts and nutritionDaily totals and history
Foods, recipes, and saved meals you createSo you can log them again
Weights you enterYour weight trend and calorie target
Height, date of birth, and biological sex, if you enter themThe starting estimate of what you burn
Your goal and calorie targetsThe number shown on Today
Barcode results already looked upSo a repeat scan works offline

Every one of those fields is optional. The app works if you enter nothing about yourself at all.

What leaves your iPhone

Barcode lookups

When you scan or type a barcode, the app asks Open Food Facts — a free, open food database run by a non-profit — what that product is. That request contains the barcode and nothing else: no name, no account, no device identifier, no history. The app identifies itself by name and version, as Open Food Facts asks every app to do.

Results are saved on your phone so the same product is not looked up twice. If you never scan a barcode, the app never contacts anything.

Nothing else

There are no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting that transmits your content, and no remote configuration. The app contains no third-party tracking code.

Apple Health

Apple Health is entirely optional and off until you turn it on. Each part is asked for separately, so turning on weight import does not also ask for permission to write your nutrition.

  • Reading your weight and height, to keep your trend up to date.
  • Writing weights you type into Simple Track, so your other apps see them. Weights that came from Health are never written back.
  • Reading nutrition totals, if you ask the app to bring across days you logged in another app, or to rebuild days after losing your data.
  • Writing what you log, so other apps can see your calories and macros.
  • Reading movement and steps, shown only as context. Activity never changes your calorie target.

Health data is used only for these features. It is never used for advertising, profiling, or sale, it is never included in a meal you share with someone, and it is never sent off your device by Simple Track. You can turn any of it off at any time in the Health app, and the rest of Simple Track keeps working.

Photos and the camera

The camera is used to scan barcodes and to read nutrition labels. When you read a label, the image is decoded, the text is recognised on your device, and the image is released. No photograph is saved by Simple Track and none is uploaded. Choosing a picture from your library uses the system picker, which hands the app only the one image you picked — Simple Track never asks for access to your photo library.

Backups and sharing

Your own export

You can export your entire history to a file. It is encrypted with a password you choose, using AES-256-GCM with a key derived by PBKDF2. The password is not stored in the file. Where you put the file is up to you; the app does not send it anywhere.

After an export, the app offers — once, and never by default — to save that password to your keychain. If you accept, it syncs through your Apple Account keychain so a replacement iPhone can still open the backup. That is the only thing Simple Track ever asks to sync, it holds a password and never your health data, and you can delete it in Settings. If you decline and later forget the password, there is no developer or server that can recover it.

Ordinary device backups

Simple Track's data is included in your iPhone's normal backup, whether that is iCloud or a computer, so a new phone restores your history along with everything else. Those backups are managed by Apple under Apple's terms, not by this app.

Sharing a meal

You can send someone a single meal or recipe as a file. It contains that meal only: its foods, amounts, and nutrition. It never contains your profile, weight, targets, other logs, Health data, or your private corrections to foods.

Deleting your data

Settings has a control that deletes every log, food, recipe, weight, and setting on the iPhone. Deleting the app removes its data too. Anything Simple Track wrote to Apple Health stays in Health until you remove it — there is a separate control that deletes only the entries this app wrote, and never data from other apps.

Children

Simple Track is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect information from them.

Where the food data comes from

Bundled nutrition data is from USDA FoodData Central, which is in the public domain in the United States. USDA does not endorse Simple Track. Barcode product data is from Open Food Facts, made available under the Open Database License.
