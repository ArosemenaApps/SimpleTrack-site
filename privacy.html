Privacy Policy

Simple Track — Calorie Log

Effective 23 November 2026

HomeSupport

The short version. Simple Track has no account system. Your food diary, weights, recipes, targets, and settings are stored on your iPhone, not on a server. Nothing is sold, nothing is used for advertising, and there is no analytics or tracking code in the app.

Your diary, weights, goals, history, and anything from Apple Health stay on your iPhone and are never uploaded to us. The app connects to the internet only for things you ask it to do: looking up a food in Open Food Facts, sharing a meal as a link or opening one, showing the list in More Apps, and buying or restoring Simple Track Plus through Apple. The one service we run ourselves is the meal-link service. It stores each meal you deliberately share, encrypted so that we cannot read it, for up to 30 days, and keeps an installation-specific identifier for each copy of the app that has shared a link. That identifier is not associated with an account, a name, or an email address. Both are explained below.

Everything below is stored on your iPhone, in the app's own storage area, and is not uploaded to Arosemena Apps. The one exception is a meal you choose to share as a link, which leaves your iPhone encrypted, as described under Sharing a meal as a link.

What leaves your iPhone

There are five kinds of connection, each started by something you do:

  • Your diary and health records — food log, weights, body measurements, goals, targets, history, and Apple Health data — are never uploaded.
  • A meal you deliberately share as a link is uploaded encrypted to our meal-link service.
  • Food lookups send a barcode or search words to Open Food Facts.
  • More Apps downloads a list of apps from this website.
  • Subscriptions are bought, renewed, and restored through Apple.

Barcode lookups

When you scan or type a barcode the app does not already know, it asks Open Food Facts — a free, open food database run by a non-profit — what that product is. That request contains the barcode and nothing else: no name, no account, no identifier, no history. The app identifies itself by name and version, as Open Food Facts asks every app to do. Open Food Facts receives your IP address, as any website does, and handles it under its own privacy policy.

Results are saved on your phone so the same product is not looked up twice.

Searching by name

Typing in Search only searches your iPhone: the built-in database, your own foods, and products already saved. Nothing is sent while you type. When you press Search on the keyboard, or tap Search Open Food Facts for “…”, the app also asks Open Food Facts whether it knows a packaged product by that name. That request contains the words you searched for (at least three characters) and nothing else: no name, no account, no identifier, no history of what you have searched before. If you then choose a result whose serving size the search did not include, the app asks for that one product by its barcode.

This is on when you first install the app, and can be turned off in Settings → Search → Search Open Food Facts. With it off, nothing you type ever leaves your iPhone. Barcode lookups are unaffected.

Sharing a meal as a link

You can share a meal or recipe as a link instead of a file. When you tap Share Link or Copy Link:

  • Your iPhone creates a new random key for that one share and encrypts the meal with it (AES-256-GCM). Only the encrypted copy is uploaded, to Simple Track's meal-link service at track.arosemenaapps.com, which runs on Cloudflare.
  • The key is placed in the link after the #. Browsers and apps do not send that part of a link to a server, so the key is never sent to our service and we cannot read the meal.
  • Anyone who has the complete link can open the meal — the link is both the address and the key. Share it only with people you want to see the meal. Someone who opens it can save their own copy, which stays theirs after the link ends.
  • A meal link contains that meal only: its name, its foods with amounts and nutrition, its versions, and any preparation times, recipe link and cooking steps you added. It never contains your profile, weight, targets, other logs, Health data, your notes on the meal, or your private corrections to foods.

Alongside the encrypted copy the service stores only what it needs to run: a random link identifier, the size of the upload, when it was made and when it expires, and a one-way fingerprint of a private code that only your iPhone holds, which is how it knows a request to turn the link off came from you.

How long links last. A link works for up to 30 days. After that it stops working, and an automatic cleanup that runs every hour deletes the encrypted copy and its record; as a backstop, storage rules delete any copy older than 32 days. You can turn a link off sooner in Settings → Shared Links → Turn Off Link: the encrypted copy is deleted at once, and a small record without the meal is kept until the original 30 days end, so the link can say it was turned off rather than that it never existed.

Links do not depend on your own data. Deleting your Simple Track data, or deleting the app, does not turn off links you have already shared — someone you sent a meal to can still open it until it expires. Turning a link off is the only way to end it early. If you delete the app, you also lose its list of shared links, and with it the ability to turn those links off yourself; write to ArosemenaApps@gmail.com with the link and we will remove it. A meal someone has already saved from your link belongs to their own library and is not affected by anything you do afterwards.

Installation identifier. To stop the service being misused, the app proves it is a genuine copy of Simple Track using Apple's App Attest. The first time a copy of the app shares a link, it creates a cryptographic key on your iPhone and registers it with Apple and with our service. The service stores that key's identifier and public part, when it was registered and last used, and a usage counter. This is an installation-specific identifier: it is different for each installation of the app, and it is not associated with a Simple Track account (there are none), a name, or an email address. The service does not record which meals were shared with it. It is used only to check that uploads come from the app and to limit how many links one installation can make each day; those daily counts are kept for about two days. The identifier is deleted after 90 days in which that installation shares nothing. It is not used for advertising or tracking.

Opening a link downloads the encrypted meal and decrypts it on your iPhone. It does not send your diary, any account information (Simple Track has no accounts), or any other nutrition records, and opening a link does not register an installation identifier.

Internet addresses. Sharing or opening a link involves normal network information, including your IP address, which Cloudflare processes to deliver the connection and apply rate limits. Our service does not intentionally store IP addresses in its database or application logs; its logs record only that a request happened and its outcome — never link identifiers, meal contents, keys, or IP addresses. Cloudflare may process connection data to operate and protect its network under its own privacy policy.

Sharing a meal as a file, which works without the internet, is still available and sends nothing to us.

More Apps

When you open Settings → More Apps, the app downloads the current list of Arosemena Apps apps and their icons from this website, which is hosted on GitHub Pages. The request contains no identifier and nothing about you, and uses no cookies. GitHub receives your IP address, as it does for any visit to this site. Links to the App Store open in Apple's own App Store sheet.

Simple Track Plus and purchases

Simple Track Plus is an optional subscription sold by Apple. Apple handles payment, billing, renewal, refunds, and Family Sharing under Apple's privacy policy; we never see your payment details or your Apple Account. To know whether Plus is active, the app reads Apple's signed purchase records on your iPhone, and the widgets check the same records themselves. Nothing about your subscription is sent to us, and Simple Track keeps no record of it of its own. Subscribing or not changes nothing about how your data is stored or shared.

Nothing else

There are no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting that transmits your content, and no remote configuration. The app contains no third-party tracking or advertising code, and it does not track you across other companies' apps or websites. Links you tap yourself, such as the ones to this site, open in your browser.

Apple Health

Apple Health is entirely optional and off until you turn it on. Each part is asked for separately, so turning on weight import does not also ask for permission to write your nutrition.

These are the Health data types Simple Track can use, each only after you allow it:

  • Reading your weight, body-fat percentage, height, date of birth, and biological sex, to keep your trend up to date and to estimate what you burn.
  • Writing weights you type into Simple Track, so your other apps see them. Weights that came from Health are never written back.
  • Reading nutrition totals (calories, macros, and the other nutrients another app has saved), if you ask the app to bring across days you logged in another app, or to rebuild days after losing your data.
  • Writing what you log — calories, macros, and the other nutrients you log, and optionally water — so other apps can see it.
  • Reading active energy and steps, shown only as context. Activity never changes your calorie target.

Health data is used only for these features. It is never used for advertising, profiling, or sale, it is never included in a meal you share — by file or by link — and Simple Track never sends it anywhere. What the app has brought in from Health — weights, body-fat readings, and daily totals — becomes part of your Simple Track history, so it is stored like the rest of that history and is included in an export only when you make one yourself. You can turn any of it off at any time in the Health app, and the rest of Simple Track keeps working.

Photos and the camera

The camera is used to scan barcodes and to read nutrition labels. When you read a label, the image is decoded, the text is recognised on your device, and the image is released. No photograph is saved by Simple Track and none is uploaded. Choosing a picture from your library uses the system picker, which hands the app only the one image you picked — Simple Track never asks for access to your photo library.

Photographing or describing a meal

Where this feature is offered on iPhones with Apple Intelligence, you can photograph a meal, describe it in a few words, or both, to get suggested foods. The photo and your words are read by Apple's on-device model, on your iPhone. Simple Track does not use Apple's Private Cloud Compute or any other online service for this, so neither leaves your iPhone. The photo is shrunk, its location and other metadata removed, read once, and released. It is never saved and never included in a meal you share. Nothing is logged until you check the suggestions and tap Log.

Backups and sharing files

Your own export

You can export your entire history to a file. It is encrypted with a password you choose, using AES-256-GCM with a key derived by PBKDF2. The password is not stored in the file. Where you put the file is up to you; the app does not send it anywhere.

After an export, the app offers — once, and never by default — to save that password to your keychain. If you accept, it syncs through your Apple Account keychain so a replacement iPhone can still open the backup. It holds a password and never your health data, and you can delete it in Settings. If you decline and later forget the password, there is no developer or server that can recover it.

Ordinary device backups

Simple Track's diary is included in your iPhone's normal backup, whether that is iCloud or a computer, so a new phone restores your history along with everything else. Those backups are managed by Apple under Apple's terms, not by this app. The list of meal links you have shared is not included.

Sharing a meal as a file

You can send someone a single meal or recipe as a file. It contains the same meal information as a link (above) and never your profile, weight, targets, other logs, Health data, or your private corrections to foods. A file goes only where you send it.

Deleting your data

Settings has a control that deletes every log, food, recipe, weight, target, and setting on the iPhone. Deleting the app removes all of its data from the iPhone, including the list of links you have shared. Anything Simple Track wrote to Apple Health stays in Health until you remove it — there is a separate control that deletes only the entries this app wrote, and never data from other apps.

Meal links you shared are not affected. Deleting your data, or the app, does not turn off links you already shared: each keeps working for the rest of its 30 days unless you turn it off first in Settings → Shared Links. Deleting the app removes that list, so afterwards you can no longer turn those links off yourself; write to us with the link and we will remove it. Meals other people saved from your links stay in their libraries. The installation identifier for your copy of the app is deleted automatically after 90 days without a share. Because the service holds no account, name, or email address, we cannot look up records by those; a link itself is how to identify one.

Children

Simple Track is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect information from them.
